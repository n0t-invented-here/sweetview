Screen captures 28 pixels around the window (frame-light.png, frame-dark.png):
the window has Windows 11's rounded corners and its one-pixel system border on
all four sides; the strip starts at the very top of the window with no caption
above it (light strip in the light run, dark strip in the dark run). The
shadow is not distinguishable from the dark desktop wallpaper behind it in
either capture. The maximised capture (maximized-top-left-*.png) shows the
strip flush with the top of the work area and its tabs fully on screen: the
frame's resize border is outside the screen, as for any maximised window.

What the live tests read back from the OS for the same frame (see the
test list below): the styles keep WS_CAPTION, WS_SYSMENU, WS_THICKFRAME,
WS_MINIMIZEBOX, WS_MAXIMIZEBOX (so Aero snap, maximise and the system menu
are the system's) and gain WS_CLIPCHILDREN; GetSystemMenu answers; the client
area is 1040 x 760 at 100 %, with the system's frame width
(SM_CXSIZEFRAME + SM_CXPADDEDBORDER) left, right and bottom and at most one
pixel at the top; WM_NCHITTEST answers HTLEFT, HTRIGHT, HTBOTTOM,
HTBOTTOMLEFT, HTBOTTOMRIGHT on the edges; WM_GETMINMAXINFO gives the default
minimum (560 x 420 page plus strip plus frame) and a caller's outer minimum
exactly; maximised, the client area is the monitor's work area; the dark
appearance sets DWMWA_USE_IMMERSIVE_DARK_MODE. Not observed in this session:
a DPI change (no second monitor scale to move to), and Aero snap by dragging
or Win+arrow (it needs the mouse or keyboard on the shared desktop).

Decision: the frame is tao's window with with_decorations(false) and
with_undecorated_shadow(true). Nothing the frame had today was lost, so no
WM_NCCALCSIZE handling of this project's was written.
